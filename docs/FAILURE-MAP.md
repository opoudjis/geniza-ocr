# OCR failure map — every way a text can fail to hold what the page prints

`[The failure map of the OCR pipeline, begun 2026-09-29 and kept current: every new finding is placed in a row here, or adds one.]`

## Why this exists

An absence claim («X is not in this item») is only as good as the text: the text must hold everything the page prints, and the search must be able to find a name in it. Until 29 September each check was built after its failure appeared, so each new gap came as a surprise. This map lists the ways the chain from printed page to search result can break, and names the check that covers each.

The chain: **printed page → scan → text → search.**

## A standing requirement: Russian orthography is decided per page, never by the date of the text (2026-10-03)

**The editor, on the post-1918 marker list (3 Oct 20:2x):** there is a slight chance that *Interlinguistica Tartuensis* 9 (2009) cites pre-revolutionary texts, and likewise Holzhaus, so a history written after 1918 cannot be assumed to use Soviet orthography; on that basis he could not make an absolute call; this was to be documented, together with the difficulty that pre-reform text cannot be judged by date, only by genre; and Ukrainian has the decimal i as well as the octal, so that letter is not a pre-revolutionary matter.

**Why date fails.** Whether a page prints pre-reform Russian (ѣ, і, ѳ, ѵ, final ъ) depends on the GENRE of the text and of the passage, not on the year of the edition:
- **Histories, bibliographies, dictionaries and linguistics quote their sources.** A post-1918 book can print the 1887 title «Международный языкъ» exactly (Neves & Pabst, *Historia Vortaro*, 2018), a 1910 letter, or an old-spelling citation in the middle of modern prose. The Tartu volume (2009) and Holzhaus (1969) may do the same.
- **Where and by whom it was printed matters.** An emigré or foreign press could keep the old spelling long after 1918 (Holzhaus was printed in Helsinki).
- **A pre-1918 text can print modern-looking Russian**, and the 28 Sep sample has a modern-orthography typescript inside Holzhaus (p. 51).
- **Ukrainian uses і (the «decimal» i) beside и as an ordinary letter.** On a Ukrainian page, і is no evidence of pre-reform spelling. Only the language table tells a Ukrainian page from a Russian one (*Ukraina Stelo*, *ukraina*, *Studia Slavica*); Ukrainian goes to its own model, not to the orus/rus choice.

**So:**
1. **The marker `ORTHOGRAPHY-POST-1918` is for genres that never quote old print**: periodicals written and printed under the post-reform spelling: the post-Revolutionary press, Soviet and post-Soviet (the editor, 2026-10-04: the post-Revolutionary press, because that also covers the post-Soviet press; first applied to cupel batch 2, the Soviet Esperanto press, which the editor ruled to be Soviet-era). It is NOT set from a year in a file name, and not on a history, bibliography, dictionary or linguistics text, whatever its date.
2. **Elsewhere the old-letter rule decides page by page**, and the rule must look for real pre-reform Russian words, not stray letters: as measured 3 Oct 20:3x, at least one old-letter word of 4+ letters that is a Russian word once folded (39/39 on the 28 Sep sample; Soviet-press misfires 103 → 5).
3. **Where the rule still picks the pre-reform reading on a text that may or may not quote old print, a blind look settles it** (the 3 Oct look: Tartu 9, Holzhaus, *Oficiala Gazeto* 5).
4. The 3 Oct candidate list built by date (an internal record, 2026-10-03, not published, and its rebuild) is superseded as a basis for marking; it is kept as a record.

## For the final documentation: forestalling brute force

The editor, 2026-09-29 17:25, judged this extremely brute force, and asked that the final documentation set out steps to forestall this kind of brute force. The corpus-wide second reading (112,026 pages, ~6 h) pays at the end, on every page, for what should have been caught at conversion. The steps that would have avoided it, to be written into the method (`docs/REPLICATION.md` and the final documentation):

1. **Measure each scan's contrast at conversion, inside G1.** How far the ink stands from the paper is cheap to measure on every page image. Faint scans get the boosted, sharpened image from the start, not a rescue later. The editor noted at 17:26 that this test should have overlapped more with G1. G1 already measures ink on page images, but only on pages under 25% of their item's median text; had it measured contrast on EVERY page, the faint pages would have been flagged on day one.
2. **Read each page two ways at conversion and keep the better.** The cost is paid once, inside the conversion, not as a later corpus-wide rerun.
3. **Write this failure map BEFORE building the checks.** Every way the text can fail to hold the page, one check per row. A row with no check is visible on day one, not found by an audit.
4. **Validate every check on known cases before trusting it.** Both coverage measures tried on 29 Sep failed on the first known set (302 partly-read pages); without that test they would have cleared those pages.
5. **Audit what each check CLEARS, not only what it flags**, with a blind sample of ≥ 40 pages judged on the image, on day one (the G5 lesson of 28 Sep).
7. **Every reading feeds the search text from the start; one reading is preferred.** The source PDF's own text layer (ANNO's, Bitarkivo's ABBYY) is a reading like ours. On 30 Sep, text missing from the search text was found only in the text layer or the layout reading — readings we held and did not search. Search the union; keep a single preferred reading per page, recorded in the files, for hand-over into PDFs (the editor's requirement, above).
8. **A second reading is not a repair.** The boosted reading added real text to 1,279 pages, but a blind check found 2 in 25 clear and 5 in 15 moderate pages still losing text. Faint or dense pages need an image-based transcription, checked blind, not a further OCR pass.
9. **Normalised Unicode, or nothing** (the editor, 2026-09-30: a pitfall specific to Esperanto that deserves documentation; the planned how-to for outsiders is to be forthright that normalised Unicode is a precondition). Before a text is searched or handed over, it must be plain Unicode (NFC), with the Esperanto letters ĉ ĝ ĥ ĵ ŝ ŭ as real letters. The traps, each found in this corpus:
   - **x-system and h-system spellings** (cx, gx, sx, ux; ch, gh): the author's typed substitute for the accents. A search for «ĉirkaŭ» never finds «cxirkaux». Convert, but only where the result is an Esperanto word and the original is no word of another language: French «aux» must not become «aŭ».
   - **Esperanto letters in a font's private slots** (U+E000–F8FF): *lg144* drew ĝ as U+F001. The text looks right on the page and cannot be searched.
   - **Ligature glyphs** (ﬁ ﬂ, and fonts that draw «ch», «sch», «ll» as single private glyphs). The worst case here was not Esperanto at all: the German quotations in the BK congress documents (the editor had not foreseen getting ligatures in German more than in Esperanto).
   - **An OCR model trained for Esperanto forces Esperanto letters onto other languages' accents**: «Milanović» → «Milanoviĉ», «état» → «ĉtat». A search that folds accents survives this; a text handed over does not.
   - **Do not restore missing accents by dictionary.** It changes meaning («reformigis» → «reformiĝis») and corrupts names («Ahasveros» → «Aĥaŝveroŝ»).
6. **One owed list, carried forward in full.** Items open on 28 Sep (the coverage re-test, the ledger bar, the misread dates, the search guide) were dropped on 29 Sep because they lived only in dated log entries.
