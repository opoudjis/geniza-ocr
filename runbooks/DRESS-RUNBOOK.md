## Holds in force

The dowse runbook's «Holds in force» hold here too, unchanged: the context, amortising and running side by side, the global redowse by its stages, a change needing a ruling logged in `../../QUEUE.md`, and what a build leaves pending written at the step it blocks as «Not built yet: …». So do its «Asking the editor» (point 8, the session's sign-off and the plain rows, stated there once), D4b (damaged OCR to Miles; it binds every pass below), D5 (what a silence may claim), D7 (the log) and D10 (quoting).

**Where the dowse ends and the dress begins:** the dowse ends when the readers' returns are filed (D6c step 6 of the dowse runbook). The dress begins with the facts file's assembly (step 7) and ends at the harvest and its checks (steps 9 to 12). Then the smelt (below, «After the dress: the smelt»). One command, `--after-readers`, runs the dowse's last step and the dress's first ones at once (step 7 below).

## The facts file and the line form

**The facts-writing template is the rule for every line:** `DOWSE-TEMPLATE-FACTS-WRITING-EO-2026-10-10.md`, in Esperanto; the readers' brief carries it. The dowser checks against it. In brief:

1. **One file per source:** `profiles/by-source/<source>-dowse.md`, built by `tools/dowse-facts-assemble.py` from the readers' returns and `dowse/<source>/facts-config.json`, headed `facts-format: 4` when the lines carry event kinds (the assembler sets it; 3 in older files). A hand-made file in format 4's line form whose sources are not items of `OCR-ITEM-TABLE.tsv` is headed `facts-kinds: 4` instead: the harvest reads its kinds and anchors as format 4's, without the strict checks of item keys and locators; once its sources are items (the light pathway, the dowse runbook D0 point 3), it takes `facts-format: 4`. Hand-made files (additions found outside a dowse, archival records read by hand) have the same form, are placed by hand, and are never redowsed (the dowse runbook, D0 point 3 and D8). Hand-made files do not go in `profiles/`: the harvest deletes there only files with its own «GENERITA» header and names any other it keeps.
2. **Every fact the source gives about people in scope**, the life as well as the work. **The scope** (2026-10-13, tested in `SCOPE-REGISTER-PROPOSAL-2026-10-13.md`):
   - **in:** Esperantists who joined before 1908; Idists who joined before 1931 (Interhelpo included; Occidentalists not followed as persons);
   - **out:** someone known to be neither, Delegation members included; but not every Delegation member was neither (Baudouin de Courtenay was an Esperantist from 1904–05);
   - **provisional:** a known Esperantist or Idist whose joining date is uncertain; someone no source shows either way, if there is any reason to think them in scope;
   - **after the chronological cutoff** (1914 for Esperanto, 1931 for Ido), facts about someone in scope are in, with no claim of completeness;
   - **doubt** means the source leaves scope open (no joining date, a date that looks wrong, an uncertain identity); someone the source places outside scope stays out; scope is never decided by assuming a date is a misprint;
   - a bare name in a list is no fact.
   The template's § 2 gives the same in Esperanto, for the readers.
3. **The line, format 4:** `- $$$ NAME $$$ (qualifier) **date** · {kind: object; +second: its object} · fact · anchor · locator · [themes] · confidence (labels)`.
   - **NAME** in the names register's main form; a qualifier only for namesakes and for every bearer but the register's default. **A line's head is always a person** (2026-10-14): a fact about a journal or firm goes under the person who acted, or is left to the themes pass; lines written under one are left out by «drop_keys».
   - **date** in the date vocabulary (17 forms, converted to EDTF, checked); `[sen dato]` for an undated act, `[ĝenerala]` for a standing state; an office undated is `[sen dato]`, and a date on an office is an instant within it; a line about a reported earlier event carries only its own act. An assertion or a «juĝo» is dated by its saying.
   - **kind** from the list in force (`EVENT-KINDS-PROPOSAL-2026-10-13.md`, «The list in force»), by the line's own predicate; an object for every kind but naskiĝo and morto, the same words each time, never a date; an intended act is coded as the act (`{fondo: Esperanta Hejmo, planita}`); «alia» only when no kind fits, with the reader's suggested kind as «sugestas: …». **A judgement or explanation of a fact takes its own kind** (2026-10-15): «interpreto», dated by the act it interprets, or «juĝo», dated by its saying, never the kind of the fact it explains (Jossinet's testament conjecture is `{interpreto: …}`, not `{interkonsento: …}` like the cheques).
   - **A person in the object takes his qualifier,** as in the head: `{renkonto: POSTNIKOV (Fjodor)}`. **An identification comes from the roster, the registers or the source's namesakes list (`dowse/<source>/candidates-<date>.md`), never from the reader's own knowledge** (2026-10-15); where they do not settle it, the bare name, and the identification proposed in the report. **Every person a fact is about goes in the kind field in capitals** (the facts-writing template's rule).
   - **One predicate per line** (a defection is two lines: `{aliĝo: Ido}` and `{foriro: Esperanto}`); **every line stands alone**; **no line break inside a line** (`\n` where one is needed; the assembler joins a broken line the same way and prints each join); the anchor is the source's own words, six or more, once on its page (a record's short entry may be shorter if it stands once in a short text). **Each fact once a page** (the reading brief: «Ĉiun fakton unufoje por ĉiu paĝo»).
   - **Written once, copied by the harvest:** a statement about several people who did or underwent the same thing is one line, under the first person, the others after «kun:» in the kind field by their roster NAMES joined by «kaj» (`{verko: …; kun: A kaj B}`); a meeting, quarrel or agreement (renkonto, konflikto, interkonsento) is one line with the others in the object. A renkonto is an exchange between them; overhearing or hearing of someone is coded by what the person did. **When in doubt, include:** a mention that is not clearly a bare list of names is a fact (2026-10-13, `MULTI-SUBJECT-LINES-PROPOSAL-2026-10-13.md`). The copies are the harvest's (below, «What the harvest does with the lines»).
   - **Labels:** an independence label on every secondary line (`sendependa`, `derivita ↤ <author year, «title», p. N>`, `sen fontindiko`); `refutas: …` where the source corrects someone, and the refuted claim as its own line; `metafakto` (the dowse runbook, D5, with 📣); our additions in ⟦…⟧. Fact lines say «AI», not «Ezri».
4. **Locators:** `<item>, PDF N (prese M)` for a scan or typed PDF; `<item>, ¶ N` for an unpaged text, the readers reading a numbered copy (`sources/ocr-work/<item>.numbered.txt`, made and checked by `tools/text-paragraphs.py`, the dowse runbook D6c step 3); copies of one work: every copy's locator, «;»-separated, the work first. Copies that disagree: the template, rules R1–R4. The readers write the lean locator `(prese M) l. K`, which the assembler expands (step 7). **Every locator in the item form** (the editor, 2026-10-20: a failsafe): a line of any facts file, dowse, hand file or our documents alike, names its source as `‹item key›, PDF N` (or `¶ N`, `l. N` for a saved web page), the item key of `OCR-ITEM-TABLE.tsv` (a web source: its saved file's name), so its fact label resolves; a source not yet in the item table gets its row (Miles's table) or, for a saved web page, its file name, and a code row in `profiles/WORK-CODES.tsv` at the same time. The old locators by text file and line (`LEsperantiste.1901.7-8.txt l.1227 p.13`, `oge.3.txt:15631`) are read by `fact-labels.py`'s failsafe (`old_locator()`) only where their stem is an item and a page can be had; a locator in prose («OGE protocols oge.1 p123–125», «same», «via editor») gets none.
5. **Confidence:** ✅ **only** where the source's author is the one acting (his own research, judgement, or statement as an act); 📣 **whenever** the source tells what another person did, said or was, even without naming its source (Jossinet saying De Beaufront was born in 1855: 📣); 📄 in older files, shown as 📣 in the profiles; ⚠ doubtful; ❓ uncertain. **Voice and doubt are orthogonal flags in one field** (2026-10-13): always one voice (✅ or 📣), then where needed ⚠ and/or ❓, each once (`📣 ⚠ (…)`); the harvest reads them all and rejects a repeated ⚠ or ❓. These marks are the line's format, not alarm symbols.

6. **Check, then harvest** (step 9): `tools/harvest-facts.py --check` (0 errors required; it also checks the dates, the event kinds, the quotations on their pages, and a dozen warnings, each pointing at a template rule), then `tools/harvest-facts.py`, which rewrites every generated `profiles/<surname>.md`, `profiles/build/FACTS-LIST.tsv` (the kind column, and the hostile-witness label's column «malamika», last) and `profiles/build/MISSING-FACTS-LATEST.md`. Work the missing-facts report: every item explained or restored, the outcome in the log.
   - **Out of scope** (2026-10-15, `ocr/method/SCOPE-REGISTER-PROPOSAL-2026-10-13.md` § 3): the check warns about every line, in any file, whose person the scope register has out; the dowse files have none, the assembler having dropped them. A profile all of whose lines are out carries the banner «Ekster la amplekso laŭ la registro de amplekso», keeps its lines, and is left out of the dowse roster (`tools/dowse-full-roster.py`).
7. **Repair rounds: no fix left unmatched.** Fixes go in `dowse/<source>/facts-config.json`, merged by `tools/dowse-apply-fixes.py`, never by editing the returns. P1: one snapshot per round. P2: each fix checked on arrival; a fix that matches nothing, or is already in effect, is refused and settled the same day. P3: overlapping fixes decided. A «replace_lines» entry names the return it was written for (2026-10-15). **The editor's line rulings** go in `dowse/<source>/DECISIONS.tsv`, keyed on the locator and a phrase of the source's own text, and are re-applied after every redowse; one that finds no line is reported. Their kinds: date, name, qualifier (the line's head), **object-qualifier** («POSTNIKOV → POSTNIKOV (Fjodor)», the name in the object and «kun:»; 2026-10-15) and duplicate. A plain row (the dowse runbook, «Asking the editor», point 8) is entered without review; the editor rules only the rest. A rebuild must report no unmatched fix before the facts file is committed.
8. **The hostile-witness label** (2026-10-16, `ocr/method/HOSTILE-WITNESS-LABEL-PROPOSAL-2026-10-13.md`) is computed by the harvest, never by the readers and never in a facts file. A line is labelled when its item (by its locator) has a side in the latest `ocr/method/ITEM-SIDES-*.tsv` (`tools/item-sides.py`: the side is the language; Occidental, Novial and Latino sine flexione are hostile to both sides; born-digital texts by their marker words or by run); the item was written in 1939 or before (its own year; in a collection, the cited piece's year); and the person, keyed by surname and qualifier, was on another side at the line's date, by their own dated crossing lines («aliĝo» or «foriro» of Esperanto, Ido, Occidental, Novial). Undated lines, disputed spans, items of unknown year and people without crossing lines get none. It shows in the profile line («⟦malamika atestanto, komputita: Esperanto 1912⟧») and in FACTS-LIST.
   - **A periodical's side is its allegiance, not its language** (the editor, 2026-10-16: some Idist journals switched allegiance before they switched language): each periodical that turned takes a side by date, from the issue where the journal itself declares its side in print, not from the first signs of turning, nor the formal break that follows. *La Belga Sonorilo* is Ido from no. 90 (July 1908: Lemaire's declaration of 5 July to the League committee printed there); *L'Espérantiste* from the May 1908 number, out 19 June (de Beaufront's declaration). The evidence is in the scouting files `belga-sonorilo-bs-signature-pass-2026-09-10.md`, `belga-sonorilo-loyalist-complete-2026-09-10.md`, `belga-sonorilo-turn-complete-2026-09-10.md`, `lingvo-internacia-1895-1914-roster-run-2026-09-15.md`, `lesperantiste-1898-1914-roster-run-2026-09-18.md`. The tool works by volume (`ocr/method/ITEM-SIDES-2026-10-16b.tsv`): the volume named «1908» is the seventh year and opens with no. 92, so it is Ido whole; another periodical's volumes are checked the same way when their lines are labelled.
   - **Not built yet:** (a) the lines whose locator names no item (the older formats) come in with the redowse of their sources; (c) a side by date within an item: the 1907 volume of *La Belga Sonorilo* (the sixth year, to no. 91 of August 1908; its first number not checked) spans the turn at no. 90, so its lines from no. 90 still take the volume's side; the side needs the issue each line cites (per-issue files, or the scan page's issue match) and the dates in a table of their own, not an override per volume. **Its test, not yet met:** it must reproduce the hand-made Devjatnin 1912 line on René Lemaire (`profiles/by-source/devjatnin-additions-2026-10-13.md`); René Lemaire's only crossing line is undated, and his dated evidence (`profiles/rene-lemaire.md`) is in lines without event kinds.
9. **Commit:** the dowser commits the facts file and the dress outputs with `git commit --only -- <paths>`, never another's staged files (step 12).

## What the harvest does with the lines

- **The copies** (the editor, 2026-10-16: not all predicates are reciprocal, but the non-reciprocal ones must be captured too): `tools/harvest-facts.py` writes a copy of a line under every roster person its kind field names in capitals, the «kun:» names and a meeting's (renkonto, konflikto, interkonsento) others alike, the line as written and the kind not swapped, so the directed kinds (influo, propagando) are copied too. Every copy is marked **⟦kopio el NAME⟧** (e.g. ⟦kopio el LEMAIRE⟧); the templates and the tools that name an older marker follow it. **No profile from a copy alone** (the editor, 2026-10-16): a copy goes only to a person with lines of their own as subject; a person only ever named as an object gets no profile. An entity is never copied to: entities go out by the scope register, reason «entity» (movement «entity», their own banner, left out of the scope warning), so that the banner, the roster and the copies all follow the register; candidates wait for the editor's ruling row by row (`ocr/method/ENTITY-CANDIDATES-2026-10-16.md`). The meetings' dedupe (the same kind naming the first person) is not used for these copies. Not wanted: the transitive case (the editor, 2026-10-16: world knowledge, for the historian to read across profiles).
- **Families and households:** in the harvest's PERSONS table, not the names register (whose pairs the biography reader treats as one thing). `FAMILIES` in `harvest-facts.py` names each group's members with profiles; a family includes its members (the editor, 2026-10-16), so the group's lines are copied to them, ⟦kopio el LEMAIRE (the Épernay family, André)⟧, the family's profile kept (families are never published; their profile is the context for the lines). A group of relatives with a row of its own is named for its head, the father by default, where the sources name him («the household of Markus Zamenhof», apart from «the household of L. L. Zamenhof»; the editor, 2026-10-16); where they do not, for the member they tie it to («the Lemaire family of Épernay beside René»); one generation's unit, not a lineage; a row only when lines about the household as a whole appear. How the harvest knows a family's member: from the family profile's title («beside René»).
- **People named only in the fact text:** `harvest-facts.py` writes `profiles/build/FACT-TEXT-NAMES-LATEST.md` at every build: a dowse line whose fact names another person by surname (the scope register's people, and the profiles of one surname or a particle and a surname; not entities, not the out) whom its kind field does not name, so that no copy reaches them; not the line's own sayers (its source's author, a contributor, the «derivita ↤» source); a surname several people bear shown as shared. A word followed by a further name, straight or after a particle, is a given name there; a surname whose bearer has two lines or fewer but which the texts name 20 times or more is left out and listed «to check» (a place, a common name or a variant spelling). Each surname met in a fact text is **in** (an initial, a given name or a title before it, or the surname in capitals), **out** (inside a periodical's title from `bpeape-proofreading/sources/derived/bpeape-entries.tsv`; an article before it, the/a/an/la; a sentence opened by it with an article after it; a preposition of place or a place's prefix before it, en/el/in/at/St./West/New …) or **for review** (the editor, 2026-10-18). The listing also skips a surname that the line's own head holds. **Not decided:** what to do with them (a «kun:» from the text, a copy, or nothing for a passing mention): the editor's evaluation, as the listing's head says, then a patch by DECISIONS rows or a redowse. **Proposed, not built:** the review per surname within a source (a reader sees a few lines and rules person, common word or place). Where a listed surname proves another person, a REASSIGN row moves the lines that print the other's forename (KELLERMAN and KELLERMANN-REED).
- **Duplicate lines** (deduping (b); `ocr/method/DEDUPE-PROPOSAL-2026-10-17.md`, signed by the editor): `harvest-facts.py` writes `profiles/build/DUPLICATES-LATEST.md` at every build: two dowse lines of one person, one file, one place and one kind whose facts (without their anchors) share 80% or more of their content words; copies left out. For each source after assembly, `harvest-facts.py --dedupe-brief ‹source› ‹tag›` writes `dowse/‹source›/dedupe-brief-‹tag›.md` (the pairs, both lines in full; create-only); one file-reader judges them (unu fakto, konservu A/B · du faktoj · necerte), its return filed as `dedupe-return-‹tag›.md`; `harvest-facts.py --dedupe-rows ‹source› ‹tag›` turns it into proposed DECISIONS rows of kind «duplicate» in `dedupe-rows-‹tag›.tsv` (the dropped line's locator and six words of its fact the kept line lacks), and, where the dropped line had the more precise date, a «date» row under it, accepted with it (a kept «[ĝenerala]» line or dates in different years only noted). `harvest-facts.py --dedupe-review ‹tag› [N]` writes `ocr/method/DEDUPE-REVIEW-‹tag›.md`, N pairs (default 30) drawn evenly across all the sources, each with both lines in full, which is kept, the reader's reason and the date carried, then the pairs to merge; the editor answers yes or the numbers he rejects; accepted rows go into DECISIONS.tsv by hand, and the assembler drops each such line. **Any bulk ruling put to the editor is one generated document** with every item in full and its locator, linked in the message, never a set of file paths (the editor, 2026-10-17).
- **Ambiguous bearers** (deduping (a)): lines a reader could not give to one bearer of a surname land in `<surname>-ambiguous.md`. **Bearer proposals are deferred** (the editor, 2026-10-18: until the corpus has established attributes to draw on): `harvest-facts.py --propose-bearers SURNAME` (into `profiles/build/BEARERS-<SURNAME>.md`, nothing applied) is not run, in the batch phases too. A prefix names a bearer only as the first name or initial of the run before the surname, a title in front left off («A. F. Postnikov» names A., not F.). When it is taken up: a bearer's established attributes, its kinds and their objects (ofico, profesio, loĝado, with their years), weigh first; the tool now weighs only capitalised words of four letters or more. **Not built yet:** the tool reading a bearer's biography, the places by period, where one exists. **The pass over the ambiguous files** is a step after the harvest of each dowsed organ (the editor, 2026-10-20), by hand and by context, without the tool's proposals: the surnames whose namesakes the organ names, the bare-surname lines placed by context (a place, a subject only one of them wrote on), by hand where the line does not decide; Lemaire, Schmidt and Kühnl first. The other ways the number comes down: the candidates list at reading (the dowse runbook, D6c step 4, `dowse-candidates.py`) and the PERSONS table at harvest. The September roster runs (`tools/progreso-roster/`, `mondo-roster/`, `cosmoglotta-roster/`) matched the surname alone and filed one unqualified line per surname and organ as ambiguous; the dowse of those organs replaces them. **Not built yet:** the bingo card's witness marks («named in the … organ») counted from each person's dowse lines in that organ, once it is dowsed (`tools/bingo-card.py`).
- **Two copies, or two texts of one work** (the doubles method; the editor, 2026-10-20: the fact files keep both lines): `harvest-facts.py`'s `merge_copies`, after the merge of spellings: two lines of one person under two items of one `COPIES` work (the locator's page inside the copy's stretch; a scan holding several works, as *Originala Verkaro* does, places a line under each), of one kind, whose facts without anchors share 80% of their content words and differ in no number but a year, are one profile line with both locators («A; B») and the label «⟦du kopioj de unu teksto: …⟧» (one `same_text` group) or «⟦du tekstoj de unu verko: …⟧» (kind 0); FACTS-LIST and the fact files keep both lines; every merge listed in `profiles/build/COPIES-MERGED-LATEST.md`, with the lines placed under each work's copies. `--test-copies` checks it. It reads only ruled locators («item, PDF N»).
- **A page scanned twice inside one item:** the second scans of one leaf are withdrawn from the texts (`ocr/method/DUPLICATE-PAGES.tsv`, status «withdrawn»; the editor, 2026-10-30: only the second scan of the same leaf goes). Facts dowsed from a withdrawn page before it was withdrawn are not redowsed: the harvest (`drop_withdrawn`, after `merge_copies`) takes a line whose one locator is a withdrawn page out of the profile where the same person has its twin on the page it repeats (its `same_as`, or the page either side): of one kind, the same year or both undated, 80% of the content words shared, no number differing but a year. A line with no twin stays in. The fact files and FACTS-LIST keep every line; each drop and each line kept is listed in `profiles/build/WITHDRAWN-PAGES-LATEST.md`.
- **Kind faults that misdate** (the editor, 2026-10-16: a date taken from a judgement's saying must be flagged, or it distorts the account): the harvest dates an undated line by its saying only under «juĝo», «aserto», «karaktero» (or no kind); a saying theme under an event kind keeps «[sen dato]». These and the facts that report a judgement under another kind than «juĝo», «interpreto» or «historiografio» are listed at every build in `profiles/build/KIND-FAULTS-LATEST.md`, for the cleanup or DECISIONS rows. The readers' rule stands: a judgement is «juĝo» (dated by its saying) or «interpreto» (dated by the act, ⟦interpretdato⟧ beside).
- **Foreign accents read as Esperanto letters** (`ocr/method/OCR-CONFUSIONS-2026-10-29.md`; the editor, 2026-10-29: an artefact of Esperanto text carrying French, German and Spanish names read with an Esperanto model, which the harvest must be aware of): ê is read ĉ three times in four, é and è a third of the time, ë ĉ, ñ and ń ŭ, ö ŭ. A name with ĉ ĝ ŝ ŭ where its language has none («Espaŭa», «Genĉve», «Gdaŭska») is the OCR's, not the writer's Esperantisation, unless the page image or another copy shows it. **Not built yet:** the harvest's check, a person's name or a place in a line written with an Esperanto letter where the profile or the gazetteer holds the national accent (é ê è ë ñ ń ö ô), flagged for the reading copy and folded for the identification, never taken as evidence of Esperantisation.

## Our own documents

The folds, `editor-adjudications-….md`, our notes and analyses are our tertiary scholarship on the primary and secondary sources, and are dowsed systematically so our judgements align with the primaries read (the editor, 2026-10-19).

- **Their lines:** a line taken from one of them names, as its locator, the primary or secondary source the document drew on (`oge.3.txt:15631`, not the document), and carries the document's own judgement only inside ⟦…⟧ as ours; a line with no such source behind it is a ruling of ours, not a fact, and is marked so. A later ruling that overturns an earlier one is written as a new line naming the one it replaces. The biography cites them as «niaj pripensoj (2026) pri ‹the source›», never by file name (the S4 template, «Niaj propraj dokumentoj»). Our judgements and the editor's rulings are written as lines of kind `juĝo` in Esperanto, dated by their saying, translated, not kept in their English wording (the editor, 2026-10-20); the source's own words stay in the anchor.
- **Locating:** `tools/ourdocs-locate.py` finds the source and page behind every line of our documents (the files named `-fold-` or `adjudications`, others by `--also`) by the item form, the old text-file locator, a work named in prose (*Ora Libro*, EdE by entry, eo.wikipedia, a Belga Sonorilo issue), a file path or abbreviation (`BS10:5326`), or «same», and writes `profiles/build/OURDOCS-LOCATED-LATEST.tsv`; `--person NAME --pages FILE` writes that person's lines with the page text of each, cut around the name, for the reader's brief, so the reader reads the page it is given. A source a document read and did not save is fetched again and saved before the pass.
- **The pass:** briefs by `tools/ourdocs-brief.py TAG N FILE…` (the rules, each line's located source and up to 30 lines of its page), file-readers over the files, about 5,300 tokens a line (2026-10-20); the reader's main cost is our own root documents, read to learn which lines were withdrawn or superseded, so a whole file with its sources named in the brief is cheapest. ### Plans and their status

**The plan** (2026-10-22; the editor: the mark is mechanical, ☞). A line whose kind marks the plan (`{…, planita}`, the event kinds' rule 1; «planitaj», «planitan» likewise) is dated by the day planned, suffixed **☞** (`1914-08-05☞`; `tools/date_vocab.py`), so it sorts after a real date of the same day (`harvest-facts.py`'s `sortkey`) and a real date founds the group (`fact-groups.py`). The assembler adds ☞ where the kind says «planita», not where it says the plan was given up, put off, cancelled, refused or failed («forlasita», «prokrastita», «nuligita» …, whose date is that event's), and adds **⟦anoncita …⟧** to the voice, the issue's date by its masthead.
