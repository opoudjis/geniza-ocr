## What dowse is, and what it answers

The dowse is the production line of the prosopography, a first-order, publishable output. Its runs are fungible and refreshed source by source. Every fact line has one source of truth: its source's facts file, `profiles/by-source/<source>-dowse.md`.

It answers two questions: **where is each person printed**, and **what may be said about the items where they were not found**. It runs a roster against a set of items (way A, the usual one) or one name against the whole corpus (way B), searching every item in scope, uncapped, each in the text its signed line prescribes.

**The two phases** (the editor, 2026-11-04): the **dowse** reads (D0 to D5, the directories, the readers, filing their returns: D6c steps 0 to 6); the **dress** («erc-riĉigo») turns the filed returns into the facts file and the harvested profiles (D6's line form, steps 7 to 12, the harvest and its checks). Then the smelt. The tools keep their names.

**A dowse records presences, not absences** (2026-10-13): erasures are a historiographic matter for the general and thematic reads. A silence is evidence only in a signed item, within that item's conditions, at the tier where the reading stopped, and only for names (D5); a theme search supports presence only (D9). A hit on an OCR page is a candidate until it is read on the page image, within the limit of D4.

The dowse layer is written in Esperanto: the rosters' comments, the context briefs and returns, the logs, the facts files. Scouting stays in English. Context returns are not shown or summarised to the editor.

## D1 · The question, the roster, and every form of each name

**The question.** Write it down first, in one sentence, in the log (D7): who is looked for, in what, and why.

**The roster.** One person per line, in `dowse/<source>/roster.txt`, forms separated by « | »; a line starting `#` is a comment, and the file opens with one saying where the names came from. For a whole-source run: `dowse/ROSTER-FULL-CROSS-ARC-<date>.txt`, built fresh by `tools/dowse-full-roster.py` (one line per `profiles/*.md`, plus `tools/cross-arc-roster-extra.txt`; it adds the **names register's confirmed short forms** of bodies that have no inner space, e.g. «lingva-komitato | lingva | L.K. | LK»). Other sources of names: `bingo-card/bingo-card-v0.2.csv`, `profiles/` and `profiles/by-source/`, the dossiers, `../CROSS-ARC-PERSON-INDEX.md`. The first form on a line is the person's label.

**The roster and the names register are different things** (2026-10-13):
- **The roster is a search list, for one run:** who is looked for in a set of items, with every *spelling* the search needs to find them on the page. Its forms are search strings, not claims of identity, matched with case and accents folded and misread letters tolerated.
- **The names register is identity, for the whole venture** (`profiles/NAMES-REGISTER.tsv`): which names are one entity (a renaming, a pseudonym, an alias), which bearer a bare name defaults to (ZAMENHOF without a qualifier is L. L. Zamenhof; DE SAUSSURE is René), what a short form means on each side («la Akademio»: the Akademio de Esperanto on the Esperanto side, the Ido-Akademio on the Ido side), how a NAME some file wrote maps to surname and qualifier. People (outside the movement and the period too), bodies, periodicals, languages; places not yet. Its forms are matched as written, case included («la Revuo» capitalised is the review). Every row sourced and signed off; applied when facts are written, harvested and grouped. Read it before the roster is written.
- **They feed each other:** the roster takes search forms from the register; what a dowse turns up (an alias, a namesake caught as «samnomo», a variant name of a body) becomes a proposed row for the session's sign-off.

**Every form of each name.** The search folds case and accents and tolerates misread letters, but it cannot guess another form. Give: the Esperanto form as printed, and the Esperantised spelling the press used for foreign names; every national form; **the name in both alphabets, always** (Cyrillic in pre-reform and modern spelling; derived forms marked `[derived]`), else the item is underspecified (D3); transliterations in use at the time; pseudonyms, maiden and married names. The search finds a surname glued to initials («E.K.Drezen») and Polish, Czech and Slovak case forms of Latin-letter surnames («Grabowskiego»).

**Initials alone cannot be dowsed.** A word of two letters or fewer matches only exactly. Find initials by reading, or by a plain search in the preferred text, and say in the log that this was done by hand. Initials that stand for a person on the roster are resolved in the dress (step 10f).

**People who share a surname.** First make sure the venture keeps them apart (a qualifier in every line, or a file each). Then give each their own line, ending with first names and initials and the titles they can carry:

    Jamin | Жаменъ | Жамен || first: Joseph, Jos., J., Jozefo, Józef, Жозефъ, Ж. || titles: M.
    Jamin | Жаменъ | Жамен || first: Léopold, Leopold, Leopoldo, L., Леопольдъ, Л. || titles: M., Sergent

A title goes on the line of everyone who can carry it. Record what a title can decide on (sex, rank, profession).

**Short names and ordinary words flood.** A surname of four letters or fewer is matched with less tolerance but still meets ordinary words; a pseudonym that is an ordinary word floods at once. Two such forms of one person go on separate lines, read and judged apart, joined again in the log.

## D2 · The run

The commands: D6d, «The search»; for the fact readers' search, D6c step 1.

- **A. A roster against a set of items.** `--items` takes item-name patterns or `@file`; check the pattern against the ledger's item names first. The first run has no judgement file (D3). Time, as measured 2026-10-13: 13 people × 9 issues, 164 s; 986 names × 11 issues, about 8½ minutes.
- **B. One name against the whole corpus** (about 62 million words, 2026-10-20). As measured 2026-10-13, on a smaller corpus: 3½–4 minutes for one form with 8 processes; 8 forms in two alphabets, about 20 minutes.
- **Workers:** `--mode shared` (half the cores, beside a parallel cupel; the default for every dowse search) or `--mode solo`, the cupel's counts (`tools/cupel_workers.py`). One item per process, so a single-item source gains nothing.
- **In both ways:** each text is indexed once (`tools/ocr_index.py`, checked by `tools/ocr-index-check.py`). The run writes the record and every hit before any exclusion (`record.hits.tsv`, `record.surnames.tsv`), and refuses to overwrite. Per item it searches the text its signed line names (for SPECIAL HANDLING every reading), the texts in another alphabet always, a born-digital item's fixed text («¶ N» if unpaged, «PDF N» if a typed PDF). **Copies** of a work are listed in `COPIES` in `tools/ocr-dowse.py`; a born-digital copy of a scan carries the scan's PDF page from the page map (`tools/bd-page-map.py`). It counts pages as well as hits. **Never cap a search:** a capped search never supports «nowhere».
- **The lock** (2026-10-26): `ocr-dowse.py` and `dowse-brief-fill.py` hold `geniza/ocr/locks/dowse-‹pid›.lock` for their run and refuse while the chain's `chain.lock` stands, naming its holder; the chain refuses while any dowse lock stands (`tools/corpus_locks.py`, Miles's; `… show` lists the locks). Refuse, never wait: when refused, wait for the chain to end, or ask Miles through the editor. A reader batch reads only its page files and takes no lock. Why: a search must not read a text the chain is rebuilding (the failure record, F1).

## D3 · Reading the record, person by person

### The tiers

| tier | what matched | how much to trust it |
|---|---|---|
| **exact** | the name, after folding (case, accents, pre-reform letters, digits read for letters) | a strong candidate |
| **near** | one or two misread letters (one for names of up to six letters), or a Russian case ending | a candidate |
| **confusion** | a measured OCR confusion (é read as ĉ, ü as ii …) | a candidate |
| **translit** | Latin ↔ Cyrillic, one letter more allowed | weak: floods on short names |
| **small caps and run-ins** | small capitals misread; a name glued to the next word | weak |
| **join** | a word broken at a line end and joined, a column split, part of a hyphenated name, two words joined | weak alone; look at the words |

### How the record reads an item

The item's strongest tier, at or above the person's cutoff, decides:

| the item's strongest tier | the item says | what to do |
|---|---|---|
| exact | **exact** | confirm on the image (D4) |
| near, confusion | **candidate** | confirm on the image |
| translit, in an alphabet the person was given no form in | **underspecified**, wherever the reading stopped | give the form in that alphabet (D1) and run again |
| translit, both alphabets given | **confirm** (almost certainly another word) | read it on the image, set its forms aside, or stop above translit |
| small caps and run-ins, or join, only | **presumed noise** | a single hit after nothing is worth a look; hundreds are not pursued by default |
| nothing | **silent** | D5 |

Do not walk down the tiers until something turns up.

### When to stop, person by person

Take the exact tier, then each weaker tier in turn; keep reading while a tier adds a few per cent and its forms look like the name; stop at the first tier that would multiply the result with ordinary words, still checking its rare forms. Rule of thumb: a surname of five letters or fewer stops at exact. A rare form can be noise; a genuine hit can live in a weak tier. In full: `docs/SEARCHING.md` of the public repository, «When to stop».

### Same surname, different people

The record sorts every hit of a shared surname by the words beside it (first name, initial, title) into **assigned**, **ambiguous**, **unlisted** and **bare**. Then: (1) **contextual reading** of the ambiguous, unlisted and bare hits against the profiles (a few by hand; more by a reader, Read tool only, its return filed intact; a resolved hit goes to its person with `assign:`); (2) **a default** where one person is what the bare surname normally means (`default: yes`; kept apart as present by default and vetted); (3) **the residue** to the record's «Shared surname» section for the editor. A default never makes the others silent.

### Writing the judgement down

One line per person in `dowse/<source>/judgements.txt`:

    Bein | stop: exact | exclude: кап, кат | exclude-as-read: kabe | confirmed: RuslandaEsperantisto1907.1 p.20 | why: …

`stop`, `exclude`, `exclude-as-read`, `confirmed`, `assign`, `not`, `ambiguous`, `default: yes`, `why`. Then rebuild from the saved hits with the judgements into a new dated record, `record-judged-<date>.md` (D6d), never a scratch path moved over `record.md`; the latest dated record is the one in force (D8).

## D4 · Confirming hits on the page image

- Born-digital pages need no check.
- Per tier read, every OCR page up to 40; beyond that, a blind sample of 40. One confirmed page makes an item present.
- What is quoted or cited in published work is always read on the image, when it is quoted; a facts line's quotation is taken from the preferred text.
- Silences need no images. Defaults are vetted apart.
- How: render the pages with `tools/cupel-look-render.py ranges` into a new folder of the image cache (D6d), never a bare `pdftoppm`, and keep the images; a few pages yourself, more by a reader (Read tool only) with a control, its report filed as `dowse/<source>/D4-CONFIRMATIONS-<date>.md`; record each confirmation in the log and the judgement file, then rebuild.

## D4b · Pages whose OCR is damaged: to Miles for vision, then a redowse

A standing rule (the editor, 2026-10-26): a page whose OCR is damaged goes back to Miles for vision, so the damage does not recur, and is redowsed; no judgement is made on it meanwhile. It holds in every pass, the dowse's and the dress's.

1. **Identify.** Whenever a reader or I find that a page's OCR cannot support a reading (an impossible date, a garbled numeral, words that are OCR rubbish where the fact or date stands), in any pass (a reader's range, and in the dress the undated pass's «PAĜO», an accuracy check, an aligner, the harvest), the page goes to `ocr/VISION-REQUESTS.tsv` the same turn: the locator key and item, the PDF page and line, what the OCR prints, why it cannot stand, which pass and return found it.
2. **Hold off.** No judgement on that line: no vision read of my own, no reader's estimate from the damaged text, no date or fact ruled from it. What the line says now stays as it is until the redowse.
3. **Notify Miles.** A paste-ready «To Miles —» block through the editor, naming the new requests. Miles recupels the pages with vision and keeps them in his vision register (`ocr/VISION-REGISTER.tsv`), for his own incremental improvements; he answers through `ocr/RECUPEL-NOTICES.tsv` (kind «vision page», the request id in «why») and marks «Miles's status» in the request file.
4. **Redowse.** The notice comes back through D8's redowse of changed pages (`tools/redowse-pages.py`), then the undated pass on the new lines (`dowse-undated.py prep --redowsed`); «Ezri's status» in the request file records it. **This lifts the hold, and it is owed before the page look** (the dress, step 10c): while the status reads «held…», `prep --page` holds the page's lines back, so a held page's look waits for the week's recupel batch (D8). A request whose page a tag redowsed is matched by the page in the sources' «redowsed_pages». Why: a missed step left a redowse's requests held and its page look empty (the failure record, F1).
   - **The kept rulings across a recupel:** `UNDATED-KEPT.tsv` is keyed on the locator with its line number, and a recupel can move the line numbers. `kept()` falls back on the locator without line numbers plus the anchor, where one verdict stands, as `apply` does for DECISIONS (2026-11-04).

**The readers name OCR damage and stop there, without estimating** (2026-10-26): the undated brief's rule 9 and the page-look brief's «Difektita OCR» («PAĜO», the reason beginning «OCR difektita:»), the re-ask text (`dowse-undated.py`, REASK), the accuracy brief («OCR difektita» in the column concerned). `dowse-undated.py apply` warns «OCR DAMAGE, NOT YET IN ocr/VISION-REQUESTS.tsv» for a «PAĜO» whose reason names OCR damage and whose page is not in the request file; `dowse-undated.py owed --list profiles/build/UNDATED-<tag>.tsv [--out FILE]` lists every page look still owed, in every source, each marked «requested», «ocr?» (its reason names OCR damage) or neither. **The request rows, collected:** `tools/vision-requests-collect.py --kept-tag ‹tag prefix› [--accuracy ‹date›] [--write]` gathers the PAĜO rulings that name OCR damage and the accuracy returns' «OCR difektita» rows, one row a page, the readers' words as «what the OCR prints», pages already in the file left out.

## D4c · Address books and directories

The directories (the *Tutmonda Jarlibro Esperantista* 1904–1910, the *Oficiala Jarlibro* of UEA, the *Adresaro*, the national address books and congress lists) are read by tools, not legiloj (the editor, 2026-10-27, on a recommendation of Ezri's; each directory inspected first for what else it carries). Their returns go through the dress like a legilo's.

- **Whose entries become lines:** only persons already on the roster. An entry for anyone else makes no line (no one-line profiles of strangers).
- **What a line says:** the place, as «loĝado: ‹place›» dated by the directory's year; the occupation where given; any role it prints (delegate, a group's officer) as its own line. **Other fields, by inspection first:** each directory is looked at before its tool is written, and a field it carries beyond these (the Ido address book's languages spoken) gets its own line of a kind settled then.

- **The search first:** the strong name search over the volumes (D6c step 1, `--strong-only`, items by their ANNO names); every tool takes its hits as `--hits ‹strong hits›` (several files comma-separated, a later one's items replacing an earlier one's). ### The join, in every tool

- **A line only when** an exact strong hit falls on the printed surname (never the forename) and the profile bears the identification out by the group's or entry's town (its longest word, never its article), the initials or the forename. One initial alone: the line carries ❓. The whole surname must be a roster name: a part of a compound one is held, a double surname joined whole.
- **Only the other volumes bear a join out** (the editor, 2026-11-01): a profile line drawn from the volume being judged does not (`directory-extract.py`'s `SELF_KEY`, set by each extractor).
- **Held, into the tool's review, no line:** a surname not borne out; two persons borne out; a near or confusion form (the near forms go to one legilo pass, `--near-input`, three legiloj over `near-input-‹tag›.md`, «sama» landed with the legilo's reason as the identification); an «ambigua» profile; namesakes of different forenames joined to one profile in one town by the town alone, in one volume; entries of different full forenames claiming one profile in one volume, unless exactly one forename stands in the profile's heading; a wife printed by her husband's name («Benoit (S° René)»), `held: sinjorino (edzino)`.
- **A profile whose heading has no forename** (ZAMENHOF): the entry that keeps the join is the subject's, the subject's forename found from the profile itself. It must stand unmarked before the surname at least 3 times more often than the profile marks it as another person's, and every other forename in the group must score at most a third of that; a profile of two men stays held (RUIZ).
- **Every guard holds back rather than guesses.** The lines: `{ofico: ‹role›, ‹group› en ‹town›}`, `{loĝado: ‹town›}` with the printed address, `{profesio: …}` for an Esperanto occupation (in lower case, its noun in -o; a workplace in -ejo is not one), `{aliĝo:}` for a category and number; dated by the volume's year (⟦la jaro de la jarlibro⟧) or the list's own date.

## D5 · What a silence may claim

- **Underspecified and confirm items** are neither present nor silent until resolved.
- **ABSENCE-ADMISSIBLE:** «X was not found in the OCR of <item> (class, signed <file>)». Never «<item> does not mention X». **With conditions:** inside the conditions only. **Not signed, or PRESENCE ONLY:** the silence says nothing.
- **Whole or in part:** cite an in-part silence with its pages. **SECONDARY** items: only that the work does not mention the person. **Copies:** each speaks for its own pages. **A journal run:** every issue silent and signed, gaps named from `OCR-NOT-HELD-REGISTER.md`.
- Every claim states what bounds it: the forms searched, the cutoff, the forms set aside. About 19 of every 20 printed names are found in the admissible class: a silence is evidence, not proof.
- **Silences go in prose at the head of the facts file, never as fact lines.** An absence found by reading a whole record by hand (a census page seen whole) is different: a **metafact**, a report about the record, written as a line with confidence 📣 and the label «metafakto» (2026-10-13; in scope). The dress keeps both as they are.

### 0. The issue dates, first, for a periodical

Every «[n]» line takes its issue's date at assembly (`expand_lean`, `issue_field`), so a periodical's issues are dated before its legiloj start (the editor, 2026-10-23), and a file is never left to its name's year by default: ANNO's 50-page bundles («Lingvo Internacia 1908.4») and a name that gives the number, not the month («LaSunoHispana.1904.10»), date nothing finer. An issue is dated by its masthead first, by the item's name (an archive's metadata) only where none reads, and in the register by Tesseract's text, then the page image (2026-10-22). A date is applied at assembly, so a finer row later costs a reassembly, never a re-read.

**The settings,** in `dowse/‹source›/brief-config.json`: a «dating» key for a source of one issue a file, or the «journal» setting's «masthead» where the brief filler cuts the file by issue (step 3); `harvest-facts.py`'s `mastheads()` reads them:
- «masthead» (the pattern of the issue's head, strict enough that body text does not match it: «la jaro» is not «Ia jaro»), «masthead_number» and «item_number» (a masthead of another issue, bled through, is skipped), «issue_window» (the lines searched at a page's top, default 30), «masthead_lines», «year_tolerance» (0 for a source of one issue a file), «first_pages» (a file of one issue);
- «volume» (the pieces of one volume, so a bundle that opens inside an issue takes the date of the bundle before it; a year's front matter and collected covers, Lingvo Internacia's «.0» files, left out of it);
- «month_file_year» (a month word whose year the OCR spoils, «Junio 1g11», takes the file's year); «number_month» {«pattern», «per_month» {year: issues a month, 0 where the numbers are not months}} (the number as the month where no month reads); «unread_between» (a head that does not read lies between the issues before and after it); «name_month», «name_offset» {year: k}, «name_first» (the file's name gives the month, or the number with a year's offset; first where the OCR's heads are worse); «no_day» (a bare number before the month is not a day).

**What the parser reads** (`masthead_parse`, `issue_date`): the date after the masthead's own words; Esperanto, French, Ido («mayo», «agosto») and Volapük months («balul» … «balsetelul», the umlaut the OCR drops; «Iul» … «XIIul»; a day before them, «12. balul 1888»); two months for a double number («Mälul-Velul 1889»); the day only where it stands clean («15a Junio», not «i5a Marto» or «152 Aprilo»); a year more than one from the file's dropped as the OCR's. It takes no day from a number after «N°», «No», «N-ro», «Nro», «Nr.», «Num.» (and their OCR forms) or «Volume», so «no_day» is needed only where a bare number stands before the month, found by listing the days the heads give. A file that begins inside an issue takes the date of the volume's previous piece. A missed number widens the issue before it to the range («inter 1903-03 kaj 1903-04»). A head whose months lie inside a register row of several months is that row's issue (`masthead_date`; ANNO does not always cut by issue). A masthead that does not read leaves its pages to the year.

**A bound volume that kept no mastheads** (*Progreso* 1908–1914, 2026-10-27; the editor: the issue map is a routine part of the dowse): one register row per volume at PDF 1 with the span its title page prints, basis «kapo», or a span inferred from the volumes either side, «enhavo»; a row for each masthead that does read; a per-issue map from the volume's own notices and printed pages may follow.

## D10 · Quoting

Quote from the **preferred text** (`sources/ocr-preferred/<item>.txt`) or a born-digital item's fixed text; in published work, correct OCR words from the page image and mark a doubtful reading «[?]». Never quote from the search text, a per-page reading or a clean file.

## D11 · Known limits

- Initials alone cannot be dowsed (D1); the heads a source prints as initials are read in the dress (the dress runbook, step 10f).
- Short surnames flood, and Latin ↔ Cyrillic matching floods most: give both alphabets.
- A phrase that switches alphabet mid-way is not found as a phrase.
- Titles with superscript letters (S-ro, S-oj) are often misread: read the context.
- Faint print, small capitals and text in pictures still lose a name now and then.
- A short form of a body that is not among a roster entry's search forms is never hit, so the context reading cannot keep it: the full roster takes the register's short forms without an inner space; others need adding by hand.
- An elliptical reference the register does not declare («la Societo») is not read as any body.
- The search's rules in full: `docs/SEARCHING.md` of the public repository; `tools/ocr-name-search.py`.
