## What cupel does

Trigger: «cupel <item>» or «cupel <batch>».

Cupel takes new scans or PDFs into the Geniza's OCR corpus and gives each item a class, so that a search of it can count as evidence that a name is absent. An item may be searched for absence only under an `ACCEPTED:` line that the editor has signed.

Items go through as a batch: one conversion, one chain, one G5 sample across the batch. A batch of one item is a batch. A run counts as clean only when it goes from C0 to C12 with no patch (§ «If a step fails»).

**The signed line** (`tools/ocr-acceptance-lines.py`, C12): corpus | pages | class · G4 | the search its class requires | the ledger's conditions verbatim (scope, sides, exclusions) | the ledger and G5 it rests on | the signature. Unsigned lines begin `PROPOSED-ACCEPTED:`, and no reader takes them up.

### The tests

- **G1, dead pages** (`tools/ocr-g1-ink.py`, in the chain). A page whose text is under 25% of its item's median, but whose image has ink in lines of type, is a page we failed to read. Threshold 0. Flagged and undecided pages go to the image sort (C9); undecided is never a pass.
- **G2, a second language** (`tools/ocr-accept.py`, in the chain). Where a second Latin-script language is present, its model's reading must exist (`ocr-second-model.py`), or a vision, hand-keyed or ruled reading; Ido requires French. Pending G2 blocks the class (branch C7b.1). The gate report's G2 column lists languages, never «pending»: pending is read only from `g2_pending` in the gate's `.md.json`.
- **G3, shape** (`ocr-accept.py`). Every list-shaped page must have its column-mode (`--psm 4`) reading, which C4 makes.
- **G4, lexicon** (`ocr-accept.py`). The median per-page share of words in the item's word list (and a second language's list where one is present: detected by the gate, or, since 2026-10-17, declared in the language table, approved by the editor): at least 0.75 for prose, 0.65 for lists. On bi-script pages only the Esperanto side is scored; the Russian side counts too where its own Russian-only score reaches 40. Its inputs come from C4, C6 (the Russian passes, C6.4.2's ruled re-reads, C6.4.3's Georgian and Armenian mask), C7a and C7b.1; not from C7b.2 (small capitals feed the search text only). Novial has no word list: the gate scores it against the Occidental list, a provisional proxy, named as such where it matters. **What the label `occidental` means:** Occidental *or any generic naturalistic auxlang* (e.g. Novial, or Michaux's Romanal): the naturalistic languages share their vocabulary, so the Occidental list takes them all. The label stays `occidental` because that is how the gate generates it (the editor ruled 2026-10-12 10:2x, on *La Belga Sonorilo* n-ro 158, Nov 1912, which the gate took as Occidental-primary and which the editor believes is Romanal, that all naturalistic auxlangs count as the same here, and that the label stays `occidental` since that is how it is generated). Read it as «Occidental + generic naturalistic auxlang» wherever a table, gate or ledger names it, above all before 1922, when Occidental itself did not yet exist.
- **G5, the audit of the classes** (`tools/ocr-g5-sample.py`, C11). Blind judges list up to 10 printed names on sampled pages; each name is searched in exactly the text the class's search uses; a page with two or more names not found contradicts the class. Per class, 40 pages (all if fewer); pass = at most 1 contradicted at 40, 0 below 40.

What the gate cannot catch: handwriting and lithograph (excluded at C1, by looking); a language in another script (hence the language table); proper names (hence G1, G3 and G5).

### The classes (`tools/ocr-absence-ledger.py`)

- **ABSENCE-ADMISSIBLE**: passes G2–G4 and G1 is clean. Absence may be claimed over the whole item, from its search text.
- **SPECIAL HANDLING**: passes only by a special route (the Russian side; a Russian-dominant item). Absence only by searching every reading of each page (`sources/ocr-rendersearch-all/`).
- **WITH EXCEPTIONS**: as admissible, except named pages (MINOR or LOST), the other script of bi-script pages, or advertisement pages ruled out; absence only off those, on the side the row admits. Or a **PART** exception: one named block on a page is not in the text, the rest of the page is (`BLOCK_NOT_READ`); the page stays in.
- **PENDING**: no valid verdict yet (G1 untested or unsorted, or stale inputs); a batch item may not stay PENDING at C11.
- **PRESENCE ONLY**: fails G4, too short, unscoreable, or ruled so (e.g. Greek). Presence findings only, never absence.

- **The sandbox (in force since 2026-10-11; record: an internal record, 2026-10-11, not published).** `tools/<name>` in this sheet is short for the full path. An approved script runs free only when called as `python3 <project root>/tools/<name>.py <args>` (or `bash …/tools/<name>.sh <args>`), alone in the call: no `cd`, no `>` or `2>&1`, no `&&`, no `$(…)`, no `nohup` or `caffeinate` in front, and paths in the arguments in full (no `$G`, no shell variables). ## Rules at every step

0. **Long runs save as they go, can be resumed, and report. BUILT 2026-10-22 for `tools/ocr-reocr-best.py` blocks, fraktur, adds and empties** (`tools/cupel_progress.py`: the page journal, `write_new`, `PROGRESS.txt`/`.log`; `tools/cupel-detach.py`: detached under `caffeinate`; tested on 20 pages of cupel17's blocks list: killed after 6 journaled pages, resumed, the outputs byte for byte an uninterrupted run's; the driver: an internal record, 2026-10-22, not published, `<STEP>.done` markers). **Not built yet** for every other long step (C4 conversion, C6 passes, the chain `tools/ocr-chain.sh`, `ocr-second-model.py`, `ocr-smallcaps-reread.py`, `ocr-reocr-best.py` columns and columns2): before cupel18's C4. (The editor ruled 2026-10-22 14:1x, after cupel17's driver died with the session and its blocks dry pass, two hours from 10:39:58, left nothing, that the way of working must change: outputs must be saved incrementally so that a run can be recovered, and he will not accept jobs that run for hours on end with no report.) Every step longer than a few minutes: (a) **writes each page's result when it is done** (to a temporary name, then renamed, so a half-written file never counts) and **skips the pages already done when started again**; the summary line is computed from what is on disk, not held in memory to the end; (b) **writes a progress file** `<batch-dir>/PROGRESS.txt`, rewritten at least every minute: the step, pages done of total, the rate, the expected end, the time of the last page; a step with no new page in 10 minutes is a stall, reported to the editor; (c) **runs detached from the assistant's session**, so a stuck or restarted session cannot kill it, with `caffeinate` bound to the run's own process (it ends when the run ends). The operator reports from `PROGRESS.txt` on every «progress» and at each step's end. No long step starts until (a)–(c) hold for it. (d) **Smaller batches** (the editor ruled at 14:1x that the problem is not keeping the machine awake but the job's lack of persistence and its fragility, and that batches must be smaller: 2,000 items sent through one 5-hour job is not acceptable): a step's work is cut into chunks, each saved and reported when done, so no single run carries hours of unsaved work; the batch itself sized so its long steps take hours, not days (a proposal, the bound for the editor: a chunk of at most about 30 minutes; a batch's longest step at most about two hours).

1. Copyright material (scans, page images, their text) stays in the untracked `sources/`; nothing of it goes to a tracked file or a public place. A PDF that lands anywhere else is moved into `sources/` at once (as *Por e Kontre*, landed in `geniza/`, 2026-10-11).
2. Keep every page image and render. Renderers use `$G/sources/page-image-cache.noindex/`.
3. Anything a later run needs lives under `$G/sources/` or the repo, never the scratchpad.
4. Read a tool's write-set (its header's WRITE-SET line) before running it a new way. Never write over an existing file: choose a new name; never delete the old.
5. One chain at a time. Nothing is rebuilt or filed while a chain runs (lock: `$G/sources/.ocr-chain.lock`).
6. A fix to a tool during a run is a failure of the run (§ «If a step fails»). A fix to a shared module does not reach a process already running.
7. Russian orthography is decided per page, never by the date of the text.
8. An absence search reads every reading on disk; SPECIAL HANDLING items are searched in `ocr-rendersearch-all/`.
9. Checkers, judges and readers are agents with the Read tool only, and they run no shell commands; they return their tables as their final message, and the operator files them. The one exception is the vision transcriber (C11a), which has Read and Write and writes `returns/bNNN.txt`. Prepare their inputs first. Decision-bearing looks use at least 40 pages, and every spread for cuts. Each return is filed verbatim with its key.
10. Vision (AI image reading of a page for its text) only where a check showed a quality problem. A transcription run (C11a) needs the editor's word; a single page read to diagnose a failed check does not, and is recorded on the board. The operator's own looks (C1) are not vision in this sense.
11. Only the editor signs, and only the editor stages commits. A batch's last ledger becomes CURRENT when the editor signs its lines.
12. Nothing in chat only: every result a dated entry at the top of `$G/ocr/OCR-BOARD.md`; every misstep a row in the failure map (`docs/FAILURE-MAP.md`) § «cupel's first batches»; every open item a line in the queue (an internal record, 2026-10-07, not published).
13. Every page number says which: **PDF page N** (the order in the PDF, `page-NNNN`) or **printed page N**; both where they differ.
14. **Every request to the editor (a ruling, an acceptance, a signature, a commit go, a stop) is a plain message, readable cold**: what is to be decided, named by what it is; what changed, before and after; why; what yes does and what no does; no step codes or session labels without saying what they are; «nothing changes in substance» first when that is so; one decision per question (numbered and self-contained if several).
15. **Two copies of one run or work: triangulate, or keep one.** Both copies go through the cupel. Then a coverage check by printed page: each printed page, which copy holds it at absence quality (`tools/ocr-copy-align.py`; the method of an internal record, 2026-10-09, not published). A gap of coverage or quality: keep both, as one work in `COPIES` (`tools/ocr-dowse.py`). No gap: keep one. The other comes off the list and the ledger: `PRESENCE_ONLY_NAMED` in `tools/ocr-absence-ledger.py`, the ledger alone, C8, then `ocr-acceptance-lines.py --withdraw` and a `WITHDRAWN:` line signed by the editor in a file that sorts after the old line; its citations re-pinned; **then deleted from the corpus** (the editor ruled 2026-10-20 15:4x that such copies be deleted henceforth, as a source of confusion, unless they differ substantially in coverage or quality): its folders and texts in `ocr-rendersearch/`, `ocr-preferred/` (and `ocr/` where it has one), its page-image cache, and its PDF off the next full list, then a chain; the downloaded scan itself is kept in `sources/` and marked in `sources/_INVENTORY.md` as a withdrawn duplicate (the editor ruled that the scans are kept, out of the corpus). A copy something still cites waits until its citations are re-pinned. Never delete before the kept copy has passed. A text match (`tools/ocr-copy-coverage.py`) is a lead only. **Not a second copy:** a re-save of a scan already held (the same photographs saved again: an archive.org re-save, a library viewer's reduced PDF) is not cupelled beside it. Compare one page (pixel size, ppi, image bytes per page, `pdfimages -list`); if the new file is the better version, it replaces the old (cupel it, withdraw the old, as with *L'Espérantiste* 1907.5); otherwise record it in `sources/_INVENTORY.md` as not for the cupel (the editor 2026-10-12 09:4x). **Lublin against Vienna: the Vienna copy** (the editor's decision, 2026-10-14 13:0x, written to Kapis of BitArkivo: for his purposes the Lublin collection raises so many problems that he always prefers the Vienna copy of the same issue, where one is available): where the ÖNB/ANNO holds the same issue, it is the batch's copy and the Lublin (dLibra) copy is not cupelled; check ANNO's title list (`sources/bitarkivo-inventory-2026-09-13/anno-alph-list.html`) before a Lublin item goes into a batch. Why: Lublin's copies can lack the wrappers (*Espero Katolika*), are composites (C1), and hold the same file under two records (cupel12, cupel13).
16. **No false alarms.** Nothing is reported to the editor or anyone else as missing, ungated or not held until the full PDF list, the ledger, the signed lines, the item table and the files themselves have been checked. File names are not structure: a file named by a number may be a binding of several volumes; a «second copy» may be the same file.
17. **One stream per external server.** Only one assistant sends requests to a library server at a time, agreed beforehand through the editor (for Lublin: one assistant makes the lists, another downloads; the editor, 2026-10-11). At the first refusal (`000`, 429, an anti-bot page) stop outright, with no retry loop. Never bypass an anti-bot check. These are fixed. **Defaults, which the editor may override per server** (to be tested and negotiated): one file every 1–2 minutes; the one test after a refusal no sooner than an hour later (the editor ruled 2026-10-12 09:4x that one assistant at a time is right, that the time span is to be tested and negotiated, and that the rest stands).
18. **Signed files are never edited.** Each batch has its own new signed file; a later line for the same item goes in a later file.

## The editor's stop points

These are part of the procedure, not failures:
- before C0: a changed language row; the census's routing of a typed text;
- C0: too little disk; a PDF whose name cannot serve as the stem (renaming is the editor's);
- C1: handwriting or lithograph; a born-digital PDF;
- C5.2: the declaration; C5.3: rows outside the batch, a DOUBT row;
- C6.1: a genre marker; C6.2: many pages sent to `orus`;
- C6.4.1: PARTLY-VERIFIED or CANT-TELL; a SHORT or COUNT lead; a page LOST or UNCLEAR;
- C6.4.2: a turned page; C6.4.3: a declared Georgian or Armenian item with nothing found, page errors or unmatched blocks;
- C6.5: another script;
- C7a: `vfrk` not winning;
- C8: a moved item; C9: pages from items outside the batch;
- C10: scope; C11: a G5 failure, `BLOCK_NOT_READ`, vision;
- C12: the signature; C13: keeping one copy or both (rule 15).
